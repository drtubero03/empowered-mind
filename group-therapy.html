Why this group exists

Something is changing. You can feel it.

Something feels different — in how life feels, what you want, or who you're becoming. Midlife can feel disorienting, clarifying, lonely, liberating — sometimes all at once. We were not meant to move through it alone. Thoughtful guidance and the support of other women can make all the difference in the world.

Midlife can feel like a breakdown — disorienting, painful, and hard to make sense of. It can also be a reckoning: a moment when the ways you've been living, relating, and moving through the world come into sharper focus. Patterns you inherited, roles you adapted to, and identities you slipped into without fully choosing may begin to feel harder to carry. Whatever shape this season takes, it can also be seen as an invitation: to listen more closely to what feels true now, what you want to carry forward, and what you want to make room for in the years ahead.

Questions emerge that are harder to push aside:
What do I actually want now?
Who have I become?
What feels true from this point forward?

This group was created for that moment.

Not to rush toward answers, but to create space for honest reflection, meaningful conversation, and the company of other women moving through a similar season of life — with depth, curiosity, and care.

What this is

A guided space for reflection, honesty, and change.

This is not traditional group therapy — nor is it simply conversation.

A Guided Relational Experience for Women in Midlife is a thoughtfully guided space for women navigating a season of change — internally, relationally, emotionally, or simply in how life feels.

Together, we slow down enough to notice what has been hard to name: the quiet shifts, the growing questions, the parts of yourself asking for more honesty, clarity, or change.

Through honest conversation and connection with other women, participants often find greater clarity — about themselves, and about what this moment may be calling them toward.

Midlife is not only about loss. It can also be a turning point — a time to reconnect with yourself, reclaim your power to shape what comes next, and live more fully and authentically.

From Dr. Tubero

Why This Work Matters to Me

I created this group not only as a psychologist, but as a woman who found herself unexpectedly changed by midlife.

Despite years of experience helping others navigate emotional challenges and life transitions, I was surprised by how profoundly this season affected me personally. Changes in my mood, anxiety, and sense of emotional equilibrium led me to question what was happening—not only hormonally, but in how I was experiencing myself and my life. I found myself reconsidering what mattered, what no longer fit, and what I wanted from the years ahead.

What surprised me most was how little meaningful conversation there was about the emotional realities of midlife and menopause—even within mental health training. That led me to pursue advanced study through Harvard Medical School's Executive Education Certificate in Women's Health and Wellness to deepen my understanding of the intersection between hormones, emotional well-being, identity, and change.

But what I've come to appreciate is that midlife cannot be reduced to hormones alone. It can also be a time of profound emotional and relational change, when familiar roles and ways of living no longer feel quite right.

I created The Midlife Shift because I believe women deserve a space to explore these experiences honestly, to recognize themselves in one another, and to feel supported as they discover what matters most to them now.

Who this is for

This group may be a good fit if you…

You feel something changing

There's a quiet shift happening — in how you see yourself, what you're willing to accept, or what you're beginning to want. You're not sure what to call it yet, but it feels important.

You Feel Disconnected from Yourself

You've spent years caring for others, holding things together, or pushing through — and something in you is asking for more space, honesty, or change.

You want to reconnect with yourself

Not the self you've been performing for others — but the one underneath. The one with opinions, desires, and questions that haven't had much room to breathe.

You're asking deeper questions

About meaning, direction, and what actually matters to you now. About the roles you've held and who you are apart from them. These may not be crises — but they are moments asking something of you.

What becomes possible

What women often begin to experience

This work is about finding your way back to yourself — gently, honestly, and in the company of other women.

Over time, many women begin to experience:

  • feeling calmer and more grounded in themselves
  • feeling less alone in what they are carrying
  • greater clarity around relationships and decisions
  • reconnecting with their voice, desires, and sense of self
  • more self-trust and less second-guessing
  • a growing sense of hope about what comes next

No promises — just possibilities that often emerge through honest reflection, emotional depth, and shared experience.

Reflections from group members

What Women Are Taking With Them

In their closing reflections, women described feeling more connected and less alone, gaining greater clarity about where they are and what they want next, and developing more trust in their own feelings and choices.

They valued having a warm, emotionally safe space to slow down, speak honestly, and recognize themselves in one another's experiences. The intentionally small, curated circle allowed each woman to feel heard, supported, and genuinely connected.

Based on participants' anonymous closing reflections. Individual experiences may vary.
