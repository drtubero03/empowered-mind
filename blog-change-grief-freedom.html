Change Can Bring Grief and Freedom at the Same Time

Change is often described as a fresh start. But even when a change is chosen, needed, or welcomed, it can still bring grief.

A relationship may end, a role may shift, a child may need us differently, or our bodies may begin asking something new of us. We can know that something has run its course and still miss what it gave us. We can feel relief and sadness in the same breath.

Those feelings don’t cancel each other out. They often belong together.

In my work with women in midlife, I hear how many kinds of change can arrive at once. There may be questions about health, identity, work, family, intimacy, or what we want the next part of life to look like. Sometimes a woman knows what she wants to leave behind before she knows what she wants to move toward. Sometimes she feels ready for something new and uncertain about how to begin.

There is no single right way to move through that in-between time. It can help to resist the pressure to make meaning of everything immediately—or to treat uncertainty or sadness as proof that we’ve made the wrong decision. Missing something, or grieving what has changed, doesn’t necessarily mean we should go back. We can feel sad about an ending and still know that something needed to change.

Sometimes there is a knowing that something no longer feels right, even before we can explain why. When the same thought or question keeps returning, it may be worth pausing to listen. What is this feeling asking me to notice? What do I already know, even if I’m not ready to act on it? We don’t have to treat every passing doubt as an instruction. But we can take our own inner signals seriously.

It may help to ask: What do I miss? What feels lighter? What am I no longer willing to carry? What would I like to make room for? The answers don’t have to come all at once.

It also helps to have places where we can speak honestly without being hurried toward a solution. When women gather and share what they are living through, something can shift. We begin to recognize ourselves in one another. What felt isolating may become more understandable. We may hear our own thoughts more clearly as we put them into words.

Moving forward doesn’t require us to feel only hopeful. It can mean making room for the loss while also noticing the possibility. It can mean taking one step that feels true before we have the whole plan.

If you’re in a season of change, you might ask yourself: What would it look like to honor what I’m grieving and still listen to what I know about what needs to change?

You don’t have to have every answer to begin listening for what comes next.
